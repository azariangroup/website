Detached Accessory Dwelling Unit Construction

A detached accessory dwelling unit adds an independent living space to an existing residential property. This representative project profile covers common planning and construction considerations; feasibility and approvals vary by site and local requirements.

Project overview

Planning a detached adu construction project

ADU planning often begins with the available yard area, access, utility capacity, setbacks and the relationship to the primary home. Construction scope may include site work, a new foundation, a compact building envelope, dedicated systems and a complete kitchen and bath.

Typical scope considerations

  • Property and site feasibility review
  • Utility routes and service coordination
  • Foundation, framing and weatherproofing
  • Compact kitchen, bath and storage finishes

A coordinated construction process

Check the site

Review available area, access, utility connections and design priorities before confirming a layout.

Coordinate approvals

Prepare the project scope around applicable plan review, permit and inspection requirements.

Build and finish

Coordinate site work, construction and interior fit-out through final inspections.
